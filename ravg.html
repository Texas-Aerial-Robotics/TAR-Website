RAVG

Our team in the RTX Autonomous Vehicle Competition. We are proud to have a team participating in this prestigious competition, which presents a unique challenge where both aerial and ground robots are employed in dynamic and strategic environments.

This year's task

Scout, hand off, deliver.

TAR participates in the yearly RTX drone competition. This year's task involves a scout Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) that will map the area, discover the landing zone, and communicate the location directly to the Delivery Vehicle, UxV (air or ground vehicle). The Delivery UxV will receive the location of the landing zone, fly or drive to it, and deliver the package to the delivery zone.

Mission sequence

  • Map. The scout UAV launches and surveys the arena.
  • Find. It identifies the landing zone from the air.
  • Hand off. The coordinates go straight to the delivery vehicle.
  • Travel. The delivery UxV flies or drives to the zone.
  • Deliver. The package is placed in the delivery zone.
The system

Two vehicles, one mission.

RAVG splits into vehicle teams that have to agree on a shared picture of the world before either one moves.

Vehicle 01

Scout UAV

The aerial half of the system. It takes off, builds a map of the arena, recognises the landing zone, and transmits its location to the delivery vehicle.

  • Autonomous flight
  • Aerial mapping
  • Computer vision
  • Telemetry

Vehicle 02

Delivery UxV

Air or ground. It receives the landing zone coordinates, navigates there while avoiding obstacles, and releases the payload in the delivery zone.

  • Path planning
  • Obstacle avoidance
  • Payload release
  • Vehicle-to-vehicle comms
On the team

What RAVG members work on.

Software

Autonomy stack

Flight control, path planning, and the vision pipeline that turns camera frames into a landing zone the vehicles can agree on.

  • ROS
  • PX4
  • C++
  • Python

Hardware

Airframe and payload

Frame design, avionics layout, power budgeting, and the release mechanism that has to put the package down exactly where it belongs.

  • CAD
  • Manufacturing
  • PCB design
  • Avionics

Integration

Test and fly

Simulation before flight, then range days: calibrating, repeating runs, and hunting down the failure modes that only appear outdoors.

  • Gazebo
  • Flight testing
  • Safety procedures
  • Systems engineering

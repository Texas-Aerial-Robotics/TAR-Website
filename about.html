About

Texas Aerial Robotics

Texas Aerial Robotics was founded in 2017 by a handful of UT students excited by the possibility of building a fully autonomous drone from scratch. That passion eventually led to TAR competing among top universities from around the world at the International Aerial Robotics Competition. Today, TAR provides undergraduate students with the opportunity and resources to work on cutting edge robotics.

What we're working on this year

From the ground up to full autonomy.

Our organization is dedicated to the design, development, and programming of a fully autonomous drone, with the goal of navigating a complex obstacle course by the end of the academic year. This ambitious project leverages the collaborative efforts of our hardware and software teams, working from the ground up to achieve full autonomy.

In addition, we are proud to have a team participating in the prestigious RTX Autonomous Vehicles Competition. This competition presents a unique pursuit-evasion challenge, where both aerial and ground robots are employed in dynamic and strategic environments.

Sub-teams

  • Hardware — designs and manufactures the drone, mission-specific add-ons, and test rigs.
  • Computer Vision — everything to do with how the drone sees its surroundings.
  • Controls — the flight path management code that decides how the drone manoeuvres.
  • Simulations — catches bugs in vision and flight planning before anything leaves the ground.
  • Business — industry sponsorship, event planning, and social media.
History

Where we've been.

  1. 2017

    Founded

    A handful of UT students set out to build a fully autonomous drone from scratch.

  2. IARC Mission 7

    Herding ground robots

    Mission 7 took a monumental leap by requiring autonomous aerial robots to interact with and control autonomous ground robots, herding them out of an arena with no 3D cues such as walls.

  3. IARC Mission 8

    Swarms and QR fragments

    Teams had to control a swarm of drones that accepted commands through audio or visual input, piece together four fragments of a QR code, and avoid enemy drones — all without GPS.

  4. IARC Mission 9

    Repair at a distance

    Fly roughly two miles to a mast simulating a ship at sea, synchronise with it, swap a defective communications module, and return — in under nine minutes, using only onboard computing.

  5. Today

    Four project teams and RAVG

    Members build claw retrieval, thrust vectoring, and vision targeting systems while our competition team takes on the RTX Autonomous Vehicle Competition.

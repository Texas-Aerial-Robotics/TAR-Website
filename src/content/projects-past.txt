Name: Claw Retrieval
When: 2025 – 2026
Icon: package
Description: A drone for autonomous object retrieval and delivery using a
  claw mechanism. The team designed a 2-in-1 landing gear and claw system
  for payload handling, a driver PCB controlling a stepper motor for the
  claw using PID control, and a searchlight to allow operation in low-light
  conditions.
Focus areas: PID control, Driver PCB, Landing gear

Name: Thrust Vector Control Dart
When: 2025 – 2026
Icon: target
Description: A drone system capable of deploying a dart with thrust
  vectoring control via a coaxial motor for precise manoeuvrability. The
  dart, controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller, carried a camera for
  real-time video streaming and self-landed on deployment, released by a
  servo-driven mechanism.
Focus areas: Thrust vectoring, ESP32, Video streaming

Name: Color Vision Targeting
When: 2025 – 2026
Icon: eye
Description: A fully autonomous Nerf gun drone system capable of detecting
  and targeting maroon-coloured objects, combining image recognition,
  autonomous navigation, and rapid-fire mechanisms to hit specific objects
  with precision.
Focus areas: Object detection, Image recognition, Drone control

Name: Herding autonomous ground robots
When: IARC Mission 7
Icon: route
Description: Mission 7 took a monumental leap by requiring autonomous
  aerial robots to interact with and control autonomous ground robots.
  Teams were tasked with developing systems to herd ground robots out one
  end of an arena in the absence of 3D cues such as walls. The ground
  robots could only be interacted with by touch: a top touch commanded a
  45° clockwise turn, and a blocking action resulted in a 180° turn.
Focus areas: YOLO, ArduCopter, EKF, 2D LiDAR

Name: Swarm control without GPS
When: IARC Mission 8
Icon: swarm
Description: For 2019, IARC changed missions. Mission 8 was again designed
  to challenge the bounds of what was currently possible in aerial
  robotics. Teams had to control a swarm of drones, which accepted commands
  via audio or visual input rather than a traditional radio controller:
  read and process four fragments of a QR code, piece them together to
  unlock a chest, and avoid both enemy drones and arena obstacles. This
  mission was also GPS denied.
Focus areas: Swarms, Obstacle avoidance, QR decoding, GPS denied

Name: Repairing a moving platform
When: IARC Mission 9
Icon: trophy
Description: Our drone had to fly approximately two miles from the starting
  location, around pylons, to a mast simulating the movement of a ship at
  sea. Once there, it had to synchronise with the mast, remove a defective
  communications module, replace it with a good one, and return to the
  starting location — in under nine minutes, using only onboard computing.
Focus areas: Precision manipulation, Moving frames, Optical recognition,
  Onboard compute

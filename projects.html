2026 – 2027 TAR Projects

Projects

One goal: aerial robots that can sense, decide, and act without a pilot. Every member joins a project team and works on it from the first week.

Project 01 · Swarm

Hivemind

A fleet of small quadcopters that fly as one. Hivemind is building a decentralised swarm where every drone runs the same controller and negotiates its own role in the air, so the formation reshapes itself rather than collapsing when one member drops out. Members work on the consensus layer, on collision-free trajectory allocation, and on a ground station that can task the whole fleet with a single command.

Focus areas

  • Multi-agent control
  • Consensus algorithms
  • ROS 2
  • UWB ranging
  • Formation flight
  • Ground station UI
Project 02 · Folding & throwable

Origami

A drone that folds down to the size of a paperback, gets thrown into the air by hand, and unfolds mid-flight to run small jobs around a house. The team is designing the spring-loaded arm hinges, tuning a controller that has to recover a stable hover from an uncontrolled tumble, and teaching it to find its way around indoor spaces where there is no GPS to fall back on.

Focus areas

  • Compliant mechanisms
  • Spring hinges
  • Launch-to-hover recovery
  • Indoor VIO
  • Weight budgeting
  • Rapid prototyping
Project 03 · VTOL

Peregrine

A tail-sitter that lifts off straight up like a quadcopter, tips over onto a fixed wing, and cruises. Peregrine is chasing the transition: the few seconds where the airframe stops being a multirotor and starts being an aeroplane. Members work on the wing, on the mixed-mode flight controller, and on the aerodynamic modelling that tells us whether a design will survive the handover before we risk it.

Focus areas

  • Fixed-wing aero
  • Transition control
  • CFD
  • Composite layup
  • PX4 airframe config
  • Endurance testing
Competition team

RTX Drone Competition

TAR participates in the yearly RTX drone competition. This year's task involves a scout Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) that will map the area, discover the landing zone, and communicate the location directly to the Delivery Vehicle, UxV (air or ground vehicle). The Delivery UxV will receive the location of the landing zone, fly or drive to it, and deliver the package to the delivery zone.

Go to the RAVG page

Focus areas

  • Aerial mapping
  • Landing zone detection
  • UAV to UxV comms
  • Payload delivery
  • Systems integration

2025 – 2026

Claw Retrieval

A drone for autonomous object retrieval and delivery using a claw mechanism. The team designed a 2-in-1 landing gear and claw system for payload handling, a driver PCB controlling a stepper motor for the claw using PID control, and a searchlight to allow operation in low-light conditions.

  • PID control
  • Driver PCB
  • Landing gear

2025 – 2026

Thrust Vector Control Dart

A drone system capable of deploying a dart with thrust vectoring control via a coaxial motor for precise manoeuvrability. The dart, controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller, carried a camera for real-time video streaming and self-landed on deployment, released by a servo-driven mechanism.

  • Thrust vectoring
  • ESP32
  • Video streaming

2025 – 2026

Color Vision Targeting

A fully autonomous Nerf gun drone system capable of detecting and targeting maroon-coloured objects, combining image recognition, autonomous navigation, and rapid-fire mechanisms to hit specific objects with precision.

  • Object detection
  • Image recognition
  • Drone control

IARC Mission 7

Herding autonomous ground robots

Mission 7 took a monumental leap by requiring autonomous aerial robots to interact with and control autonomous ground robots. Teams were tasked with developing systems to herd ground robots out one end of an arena in the absence of 3D cues such as walls. The ground robots could only be interacted with by touch: a top touch commanded a 45° clockwise turn, and a blocking action resulted in a 180° turn.

  • YOLO
  • ArduCopter
  • EKF
  • 2D LiDAR

IARC Mission 8

Swarm control without GPS

For 2019, IARC changed missions. Mission 8 was again designed to challenge the bounds of what was currently possible in aerial robotics. Teams had to control a swarm of drones, which accepted commands via audio or visual input rather than a traditional radio controller: read and process four fragments of a QR code, piece them together to unlock a chest, and avoid both enemy drones and arena obstacles. This mission was also GPS denied.

  • Swarms
  • Obstacle avoidance
  • QR decoding
  • GPS denied

IARC Mission 9

Repairing a moving platform

Our drone had to fly approximately two miles from the starting location, around pylons, to a mast simulating the movement of a ship at sea. Once there, it had to synchronise with the mast, remove a defective communications module, replace it with a good one, and return to the starting location — in under nine minutes, using only onboard computing.

  • Precision manipulation
  • Moving frames
  • Optical recognition
  • Onboard compute
